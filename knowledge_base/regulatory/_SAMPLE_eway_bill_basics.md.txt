# E-Way Bill Requirements for Goods Movement in India

> SAMPLE DOCUMENT — included to demonstrate and test the ingestion pipeline.

## When an e-way bill is required

An e-way bill must be generated before the movement of goods where the consignment value exceeds
Rs. 50,000. This applies to inter-state movement and, in most states, to intra-state movement above
the same threshold. The consignment value is the invoice value including GST but excluding the value
of exempt goods carried in the same conveyance.

Movement covered includes supply, reasons other than supply (such as branch transfer or job work),
and inward supply from an unregistered person.

## Who generates it

The registered person causing the movement generates Part A of the form. Where goods are handed to
a transporter for movement by road, the transporter generates Part B with the vehicle number. If the
supplier has not generated the e-way bill, the transporter carrying the consignment is required to
do so.

## Validity periods by distance

Validity is calculated from the time Part B is first entered. For regular cargo, one day is allowed
for every 200 km or part thereof. For over-dimensional cargo, one day is allowed for every 20 km or
part thereof.

A consignment moving 2,100 km by road therefore carries a validity of eleven days for regular cargo.
Validity may be extended within eight hours before or after expiry where transit is delayed by
exceptional circumstances such as natural calamity, law and order disruption, or vehicle breakdown.

## Documents to carry

The person in charge of the conveyance must carry the invoice or bill of supply or delivery challan,
and a copy of the e-way bill number in physical or electronic form. For rail, air, or vessel
movement, the e-way bill must be produced at the time of delivery rather than during transit.

## Exemptions relevant to bulk commodities

No e-way bill is required where goods are transported by a non-motorised conveyance, where goods
move from a port, airport, air cargo complex, or land customs station to an inland container depot
or container freight station for customs clearance, or where the goods are specified in the exempt
annexure to the rules.

## Steel and metals movement notes

Steel long products such as TMT bars are commonly moved in full truck loads on multi-axle vehicles
with payload in the range of 21 to 30 tonnes depending on axle configuration. Rail movement is
usually economical only at rake-load quantities, which for most commodities requires approximately
2,500 to 3,800 tonnes depending on wagon type and the commodity's loading density. Below rake load,
road transport typically wins on both cost and door-to-door transit time despite a higher per-tonne
kilometre rate, because part-rake rail movement incurs aggregation delay, terminal handling, and
double handling at both ends.
